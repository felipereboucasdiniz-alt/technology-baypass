# Technology Bypass: Executive One-Pager

## The Big Idea

**Technology Bypass** = Using technology to make cooperation, peace, or market participation individually rational—not by forcing people to behave better, but by changing the game so that "doing the right thing" is also "doing the smart thing."

---

## Three Types of Coordination Failures

### 1. **PRISONER'S DILEMMA** (Everyone wins if we cooperate, but I win more if I cheat)

**Examples:** Climate change, corruption, tax evasion, antibiotic overuse

**Without intervention:** Everyone cheats → everyone loses

**Technology solution:** Make cheating detectable and punishable
- **ProZorro (Ukraine):** Public blockchain for procurement → bribery visible → saved $1.9B
- **Satellite methane monitoring:** Detect leaks from space → companies fix them to avoid fines
- **Pix (Brazil):** Digital payments create audit trail → tax evasion falls

---

### 2. **ZERO/NEGATIVE-SUM GAME** (I only win if you lose, or we both lose)

**Examples:** War, terrorism, crime, patent litigation

**Without intervention:** Destructive conflict

**Technology solution:** Make peace more profitable than war
- **Satellite surveillance:** Can't surprise-attack → war becomes more expensive/risky
- **Smart sanctions:** Instant financial penalties → aggression costs more
- **Trade networks:** Deeper integration → opportunity cost of war rises
- **Resilient infrastructure:** Attacks cause less damage → less tempting target

---

### 3. **FRICTION/RIGIDITY** (We both win from trade, but can't find each other or trust each other)

**Examples:** Unemployment + unfilled jobs, idle Uber drivers + waiting passengers, organ donors + recipients

**Without intervention:** "Missing markets"—beneficial trades don't happen

**Technology solution:** Reduce transaction costs to near-zero
- **Uber:** GPS + real-time matching → no more cruising for fares
- **Pix:** Instant payments, zero fee → 76% of Brazilians adopted in 3 years
- **ProZorro:** Transparent bidding → firms trust the process, more participation

---

## The Five Technology Channels (How it Works)

| Channel | Symbol | Prisoner's Dilemma | Zero-Sum Game | Friction Problem |
|---------|--------|-------------------|---------------|------------------|
| **Monitoring** (detection/info) | t_p | Catch cheaters → raises cost of defection | Reveal intentions → prevent miscalculation wars | Identify quality counterparties → enable matching |
| **Automated Sanctions** | t_s | Instant punishment → defection not worth it | Credible retaliation → deters aggression | Escrow/guarantees → reduce counterparty risk |
| **Cost Reduction** | t_c | Cooperation becomes cheaper than cheating | Peace cheaper than war | Transaction cost below gains from trade |
| **Network Effects** | t_k | More cooperators → higher returns → self-reinforcing | Larger trade network → shifts from zero-sum to positive-sum | More users → better matches → liquidity spiral |
| **Resilience** | t_h | (Less relevant) | Lower attack payoff → peace more stable | Lower downside risk → more participation |

---

## The Multiplier Effect

**Key Insight:** These channels multiply each other's effectiveness.

Example: **Methane Monitoring**
- Better satellites (t_p ↑) mean you can detect 90% of leaks instead of 50%
- This allows you to reduce required fines (t_s ↓) by 80% and still get compliance
- Meanwhile, cheaper detection equipment (t_c ↓) makes voluntary compliance easier
- **Result:** Cooperation becomes Nash equilibrium with modest penalties

Formula (simplified):
```
Cooperation becomes rational when:
(Monitoring × Sanctions) > (Temptation to Cheat) − (Cost Reduction + Network Benefits)
```

## When It Fails: Three Failure Modes

### 1. **HETEROGENEITY** (High-θ Problem)
Some agents are too costly/ideological to reach with technology

**Example:** Satellite monitoring works for large industrial methane emitters, but 80% of facilities are too small to detect

**Solution:** Target the low-hanging fruit (20% of emitters = 70% of emissions), accept incomplete coverage

---

### 2. **TRANSITION COSTS** (κ Problem)
Upfront investment exceeds political/corporate time horizon

**Example:** Kenya's IFMIS procurement system ($200M) sabotaged by vested interests before benefits materialized

**Solution:** Phase rollout, get quick wins first, secure multi-year funding

---

### 3. **ARMS RACE** (t^A vs t^D)
Adversaries deploy counter-technologies (encryption vs. surveillance, deepfakes vs. verification)

**Example:** Financial regulation: blockchain transparency vs. privacy coins

**Solution:** Focus on channels with defensive advantage (resilience, network lock-in, cost compression in legal alternatives)

## Decision Framework for Leaders

### Step 1: Diagnose Your Problem Type

| Symptoms | Likely Type | Priority Channels |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Everyone could cooperate but doesn't (defection dominant) | Prisoner's Dilemma | t_p (monitoring), t_s (sanctions) |
| Conflict/litigation despite cheaper alternatives | Zero-Sum | t_p (transparency), t_k (trade), t_h (resilience) |
| Willing buyers + sellers, but no transactions | Friction | t_c (cost reduction), t_k (network effects) |

### Step 2: Exploit Complementarities

Don't optimize channels in isolation. A 50% boost in monitoring (t_p) + 50% boost in sanctions (t_s) > 2× either alone.

**Practical questions:**
1. Which channel has the highest elasticity in my context? (Run sensitivity analysis)
2. Where are the multiplier effects strongest? (Invest there first)
3. What's the break-even adoption rate? (Often 40-60% for network effects)

### Step 3: Accept Strategic Incompleteness

The last 20% of adoption often costs 80% of resources.

**Example:** Pix hit 76% of Brazilians—that's enough for network effects. Don't waste money chasing the last 24%.

**Rule:** Optimize for 80% coverage if marginal cost of full adoption exceeds marginal benefit.

## The Paradigm Shift

### Old Approach:
"How do we **force** people to do the right thing?"
→ Regulation, enforcement, appeals to altruism

**Problems:**
- Regulation is expensive to enforce (especially in weak-governance contexts)
- Altruism doesn't scale (works in tight communities, not globally)
- Centralized control creates corruption opportunities

---

### New Approach (Technology Bypass):
"How do we make the right thing the **individually rational** thing?"
→ Engineer incentives so cooperation/peace/market participation is a Nash equilibrium

**Advantages:**
- Self-enforcing (don't need massive bureaucracy)
- Scales globally (technology spreads faster than institutions)
- Resilient (no single point of failure)

---

## One-Sentence Summary

**Technology Bypass transforms games with socially inefficient equilibria into games where the efficient outcome is individually rational—using monitoring, sanctions, cost reduction, network effects, and resilience to align private incentives with social welfare.**
