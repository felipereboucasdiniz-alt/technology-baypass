# Technology Bypass: Complete Framework Documentation

## Navigation Guide

This repository contains a complete, unified framework for understanding and applying **Technology Bypass** across different types of coordination failures. The framework is organized into four main documents, each serving a different purpose.

**Who should read this:** Academics, researchers, policy analysts

**What it contains:**
- Formal definition of Technology Bypass

## 🔑 Key Concepts Quick Reference

### The Five Technology Channels

| Symbol | Name | What it does | Example technology |
|--------|------|--------------|-------------------|
| **t_p** | Monitoring / Information | Detects behavior, reduces information asymmetry | Satellites, blockchain, GPS, sensors |
| **t_s** | Automated Sanctions | Makes punishment instant and credible | Smart contracts, auto-disqualification, escrow |
| **t_c** | Cost Reduction | Lowers cost of desired behavior | Modular devices, zero-fee platforms, mobile apps |
| **t_k** | Network Effects | Makes participation more valuable as more join | Open APIs, interoperability, critical mass |
| **t_h** | Resilience / Attenuation | Reduces damage from undesired behavior | Redundancy, decentralization, rapid recovery |

---

### The Three Problem Types

| Type | Structure | Example | Priority Channels |
|------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Prisoner's Dilemma** | Cooperation is best, but defection dominates | Climate, corruption, tax evasion | t_p (monitoring), t_s (sanctions) |
| **Zero/Negative-Sum** | Conflict redistributes or destroys value | War, terrorism, crime | t_k (trade), t_h (resilience), t_p (transparency) |
| **Friction** | Mutually beneficial trades fail due to transaction costs | Unemployment, Uber, organ donation | t_c (cost reduction), t_k (network effects) |

---

### The Three Failure Modes

| Failure Mode | Cause | Warning Sign | Fix |
|--------------|-------|--------------|-----|
| **Heterogeneity** | Wide variance in agent costs (θ) | Last 20% of agents cost 80% of resources | Strategic abandonment (accept <100% coverage) |
| **Transition Costs** | Upfront investment too high relative to benefits | ROI payback >3 years | Phase rollout, secure long-term funding |
| **Arms Race** | Adversaries deploy counter-technologies | Adaptive, well-resourced opponents | Focus on defensive tech (t_h, t_k), make legal cheap (t_c) |

## 💡 Common Questions

### Q1: "Is Technology Bypass just another word for regulation?"
**No.** Regulation typically involves:
- Centralized enforcement (government monitors and punishes)
- Command-and-control (do X or face penalty Y)
- Relies on state capacity

Technology Bypass:
- Often self-enforcing (technology does the monitoring/punishment)
- Changes incentives so the desired behavior is individually rational (not forced)
- Can work even in weak-governance contexts (e.g., M-Pesa in Kenya)

**They're complementary:** Technology makes regulation cheaper and more effective (e.g., satellites make methane regulation enforceable).

---

### Q2: "Can the same technology solve multiple problem types?"
**Yes!** Example: **Pix (Brazil)** addresses:
- **Prisoner's Dilemma:** Digital audit trail reduces tax evasion (defection becomes detectable)
- **Friction:** Zero-fee instant transfers eliminate transaction costs (market clearing)
- **Zero-Sum:** Less cash reduces robbery targets (conflict becomes less profitable)

This is why the unified framework is valuable—it helps you identify multi-purpose interventions.

---

### Q3: "What if my problem doesn't fit neatly into one of the three types?"
**Many real-world problems are hybrid.** Example:
- Climate change is partly Prisoner's Dilemma (countries defect on emissions), partly Friction (clean tech markets don't function well), and partly Zero-Sum (distributional conflicts over who pays for adaptation)

**Solution:** Use the framework to decompose your problem:
1. Which aspects are Prisoner's Dilemma? → Prioritize t_p, t_s
2. Which aspects are Friction? → Prioritize t_c, t_k
3. Which aspects are Zero-Sum? → Prioritize t_k (create positive-sum alternatives), t_h (reduce conflict payoff)

Then design a multi-channel intervention.

---

### Q4: "How do I know which technology channel to invest in first?"
**Use the elasticity analysis from Practical Playbook Phase 2.**

General rules of thumb:
- **For Prisoner's Dilemmas:** t_p (monitoring) usually has highest elasticity—invest there first
- **For Zero-Sum:** t_k (trade/network) usually highest—create positive-sum alternatives
- **For Friction:** t_c (cost reduction) and t_k (network) are tied—invest in both together (they multiply)

But **always calibrate to your context** using sensitivity analysis.

---

### Q5: "What's the typical ROI for Technology Bypass interventions?"
**Highly variable by domain:**
- **Prisoner's Dilemmas (corruption, climate):** 50:1 to 500:1 at scale (ProZorro, methane monitoring)
- **Friction (markets, payments):** 20:1 to 200:1 (Pix, Uber)
- **Zero-Sum (conflict prevention):** Hard to monetize, but potentially 10:1 to 100:1 if war avoided

**Payback period:**
- Friction problems: 6 months to 2 years (fast)
- Prisoner's Dilemmas: 1 to 3 years (medium)
- Zero-Sum: 5 to 10 years (slow, but high stakes)

See **Practical Playbook Table D** for benchmarks.

---

### Q6: "Can Technology Bypass work in low-tech or low-governance environments?"
**Yes, with caveats:**

**Success stories:**
- **M-Pesa (Kenya):** Mobile money in context of weak financial regulation—worked because of network effects (t_k) and low cost (t_c)
- **ProZorro (Ukraine):** E-procurement in context of endemic corruption—worked because of civil society monitoring (t_p) and public transparency

**Requirements:**
1. At least ONE technology channel must be deployable (doesn't need to be all five)
2. Must overcome vested interests (coalition-building is critical)
3. May require longer time horizons (transition costs are higher in low-capacity contexts)

**Key insight:** Technology Bypass can sometimes work *better* in weak-governance contexts because it doesn't rely on state capacity for enforcement (e.g., blockchain creates trust without centralized authority).

---

### Q7: "What are the ethical concerns with Technology Bypass?"
**Valid concerns include:**

1. **Surveillance:** High t_p (monitoring) can threaten privacy (e.g., satellite tracking, blockchain tracing)
   - **Mitigation:** Transparent governance, data minimization, privacy-by-design

2. **Exclusion:** High-θ agents (costly to reach) may be strategically abandoned
   - **Mitigation:** Equity analysis, targeted subsidies for vulnerable groups, complementary social programs

3. **Lock-in:** Strong t_k (network effects) can create monopolies
   - **Mitigation:** Open standards, interoperability requirements, neutral governance

4. **Automation bias:** Over-reliance on t_s (automated sanctions) can remove human judgment
   - **Mitigation:** Appeals processes, human-in-the-loop for edge cases

**See Engineering-Cooperation-HBR.md "Beware of Lock-In and Privacy Risks"** for detailed discussion.

## 🚀 Getting Started: Three Simple Steps

### 1. Identify Your Problem Type (5 minutes)
Use the decision tree in **executive-summary.md** or **practical-playbook.md** Phase 1.1.

Ask:
- Is everyone worse off than they could be, but no one changes? → **Prisoner's Dilemma**
- Is there destructive conflict despite cheaper alternatives? → **Zero-Sum**
- Are there willing buyers + sellers, but no transactions? → **Friction**

---

### 2. Check Your Technology Readiness (10 minutes)
Rate your current levels of t_p, t_s, t_c, t_k, t_h (scale 0-1).

Use the table in **practical-playbook.md** Step 1.4.

Identify which channels have the most room for improvement.

---

### 3. Estimate Potential Impact (15 minutes)
Use benchmarks from **executive-summary.md** Table D:
- Prisoner's Dilemma interventions typically yield 50:1 to 500:1 ROI
- Friction interventions typically yield 20:1 to 200:1 ROI
- Zero-Sum interventions are harder to monetize but can avoid catastrophic costs

If your back-of-the-envelope ROI >10:1, proceed with full diagnostic (Phase 1 of Practical Playbook).

---

**Total time to initial assessment: 30 minutes**

If it looks promising, commit to the full 4-phase process (18-36 months from diagnosis to full deployment).
